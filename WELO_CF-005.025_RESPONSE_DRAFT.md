**Q1. What are the qualifications and background of the Focus Group Moderators?**

Cognitive debriefing interviewers ("moderators") qualify under SOP-019 §6 as domain specialists. Each is on

remotely depending on the target population, locale and sponsor requirement — remote where respondents are
geographically dispersed, in-person where the population or instrument requires it. Where a sponsor protocol
specifically requires group sessions we can run them, typically 5–8 participants per group.

**Q4. How are members of focus groups recruited? What selection criteria apply? Healthy volunteers, patients
or both? If patients, recruited for a specific therapeutic area/disease?**

Respondents are recruited to the screening criteria agreed with the client in the pre-fieldwork protocol
(SOP-008 §5.1, §7). Recruitment is performed by the interviewer or by a recruitment consultant under NDA
(SOP-019 §6.5). Both healthy volunteers and patients are used, determined by the instrument: general
population or generic HRQoL instruments are debriefed with healthy volunteers representative of the general
population; disease-specific instruments are debriefed with patients who have the relevant condition, as
SOP-008 §6 requires respondents to be genuine target-population members. Where patients are recruited, they
are recruited for the specific therapeutic area or disease the instrument addresses. Recruitment and
de-identified respondent demographics are documented for every project.

**Q5. How do you ensure focus group members are a representative sample of the target population?**

Representativeness is defined in the agreed protocol before fieldwork, not after. Screening criteria cover
the characteristics that matter for comprehension — age range, sex, education or literacy level, and for
disease-specific instruments the condition and where relevant disease stage or treatment experience —
together with country and language variety. The interviewer screens each respondent against these criteria
and records a de-identified respondent profile per interview (SOP-008 §5.4). Internal QA verifies at review
that the achieved sample met the agreed criteria and scope before the report is released (SOP-008 §6); a
shortfall is returned to the interviewer rather than reported around.

**Q6. What process does the moderator follow to gather feedback and produce the cognitive debriefing report?**

The interviewer administers the translated instrument, then conducts a structured cognitive interview using
a probe guide — client-supplied where provided — covering comprehension, clarity and cultural
appropriateness item by item. Every interview is documented: probes, responses and a de-identified
respondent profile; SOP-008 §6 prohibits undocumented interviews. The interviewer then analyses across
respondents and drafts the report: item-level comprehension findings, problematic items, proposed
refinements and a summary, with **every finding traceable to the interview evidence that produced it**. The
report and supporting documentation are uploaded to the workflow step, then reviewed and signed off by an
internal QA reviewer independent of the interviewer before release.

**Q7. Are interviews always simultaneous with all participants as a focus group, or sometimes individual?
When would you recommend one over the other?**

We conduct cognitive debriefing as **individual interviews by default**, and this is our recommendation for
comprehension testing. Cognitive debriefing measures whether an individual respondent understands an item as
intended; in a group, dominant participants and social conformity contaminate that signal, and item-level
findings can no longer be traced to a single respondent's comprehension. Individual interviews also protect
confidentiality where respondents are patients discussing their condition. Group sessions can be
appropriate where a sponsor protocol requires them, or for exploratory work on cultural acceptability of
terminology rather than item comprehension. Where a sponsor requests groups we will run them and note the
methodological trade-off in the report.

**Q8. Do you use your own template for the cognitive debriefing report? Can you use a client template?**

We maintain our own controlled report template covering respondent sample and de-identified demographics,
methodology, item-level findings with traceability to interview evidence, proposed refinements and summary.
Yes — we routinely work to client and sponsor templates and will use the Welocalize or sponsor template
where one is provided; the deliverable format is agreed at intake as part of the pre-production
specification (SOP-008 §7).

## 4. IT and Compliance

**Q1. How do you store electronic records? Do you have a formal data backup policy?**

All project and quality records are held in the Cethos portal on managed cloud infrastructure with encryption
in transit and at rest, plus Microsoft 365/SharePoint and AWS S3 for controlled document and file storage;
systems, purposes and data residency are registered in REG-SP-001. Yes — backup is governed by SOP-016 *Data
Backup and Recovery*, which supersedes the former standalone policy: point-in-time recovery on the database
plus an independent AWS S3 replica. Backups are verified (CTS-REC-BKP-001) and restores are actually tested,
including an isolated-environment DR restore (CTS-REC-RST-002/003/004). Business continuity is covered by
SOP-017 with a tabletop exercise record (CTS-REC-BCT-001).

**Q2. How do you ensure data integrity?**

By system-enforced controls rather than procedure alone. Row-level security is enabled on 386 of 387
database tables with three-tier role-based access, OTP authentication and no shared accounts (SOP-014).
Quality and communication records are append-only and hash-chained — UPDATE and DELETE are blocked at the
database, and the order Communication & Lifecycle Log is integrity-verifiable on demand via
`verify_order_comm_log_integrity()`. SOP versions are immutable once approved; every document action is
written to an append-only audit log. Controls are designed to ALCOA+ principles and were examined during the
IQVIA qualification audit in June 2026. Change control over the platform follows SOP-026 and SOP-027.

**Q3. What is your retention period for electronic records?**

Project and quality records are retained for a **minimum of five years** in line with ISO 17100:2015 §6.2,
and longer where a sponsor contract or applicable regulation requires it — sponsor-specified retention
prevails. Retention, archival and secure destruction are governed by SOP-001 and SOP-014, with the position
set out in STMT-001 *Inspection History & Records Retention Statement* (Attachment 6). Identifiable patient
data is retained only as long as necessary for the agreed purpose and is then securely deleted; deliverables
are de-identified.

**Q4. Please describe your information security policy.**

SOP-014 *Data Security and Confidentiality* governs information security: role-based least-privilege access
with row-level enforcement, OTP authentication, no shared accounts, encryption in transit and at rest, and
confidentiality binding for every member of staff and every supplier through executed NDAs. Client and
patient data is confined to controlled storage systems and is never routed to AI, OCR or unmanaged email
(SOP-019 §6.6). Sub-processors are assessed and registered under SOP-018 and REG-SP-001. Supporting controls
cover backup and recovery (SOP-016), business continuity (SOP-017), change control (SOP-026, SOP-027) and
offboarding with data return or deletion (SOP-040). Security risks are tracked in REG-RISK-001 and reviewed
at Management Review.

**Q5. What is your process for electronic signature? Do you have a CFR Part 11 signature tool?**

Yes for the COA-relevant scope. Controlled documents are approved through the Cethos portal's three-signature
electronic sign-off — prepared, independently reviewed and approved by distinct authenticated staff — each
